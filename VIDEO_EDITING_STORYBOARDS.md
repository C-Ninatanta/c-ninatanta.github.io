# Short video highlight edit plans

These are **shot-list suggestions**, not claims that footage already exists. Both films are designed to be **silent or natural-sound clips with legible on-screen labels** rather than voiceovers. Aim for **45–60 seconds** if possible, within the preferred 30–90-second range.

## 1. Apple-harvesting gripper — physical mechanism

**Goal:** Make the mechanism understandable at a glance: what moves, what drives the movement, how compliant fingers contact an apple.

| Approx. time | Visual to capture | Short on-screen label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–7 s | Steady close-up of the physical gripper, all three fingers visible | `Three compliant fingers` |
| 7–22 s | Side/front view showing cable-driven closing, preferably one continuous motion | `Cable-driven closing` |
| 22–45 s | Clear view of a representative apple contact/grasp sequence | `Apple contact` |
| 45–60 s | Fingers reopening, mechanism returning to its resting position | `Opening and reset` |

**Optional footage:** Close-up of the pulley / cable routing or a slower repeat of the actuation, if it can be filmed clearly. Avoid adding diagrams unless you actually have matching footage or technical figures. The published success rate should not be presented as if it were measured by a single demo clip.

## 2. Wearable haptic feedback — wearable hardware

**Goal:** Show the physical device, where it sits on the operator, and what can be observed when it is operating. Do not imply the camera records the user's tactile sensation.

| Approx. time | Visual to capture | Short on-screen label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–7 s | Well-lit view of the complete device on a neutral surface | `Device overview` |
| 7–22 s | Actual operator putting on / fastening / positioning the device | `How it is worn` |
| 22–45 s | Close-up of the real feedback-generating components while active, **only if their motion/response is visibly demonstrable** | `Feedback components` |
| 45–60 s | Wider view of the real device being worn during a genuine test | `Worn during testing` |

**If the feedback mechanism is not visible:** replace the close-up with an honest hardware detail shot and use `Hardware close-up` rather than claiming visible actuation. Use no numerical outcome claims without a verified source. Obtain permission before filming identifiable research participants.

## Visual guidelines

- **Format:** 16:9 horizontal video, ideally 1920×1080, MP4 export with H.264; YouTube Public or Unlisted works best for embedding.
- **Pacing:** 4–6 focused shots, no rapid montage. Use stable lighting and camera placement; preserve the real physical mechanism.
- **Captions:** White text on a high-contrast semi-transparent dark strip; 2–5 words per label; keep labels away from the hardware; show each label long enough to read.
- **Branding:** Small muted crimson (`#A60F2D`) accent is sufficient. Avoid logos, elaborate transitions, fake animated grids, and synthetic robot imagery.
- **Audio:** Natural lab audio or silence. Subtitles are needed only if speech is included; don't rely on audio to explain motion.
- **End card:** A brief project title and your site URL, if desired. No unsupported numerical claims.

## Upload and embed

1. Upload your final highlight as **Unlisted** or Public on YouTube, or provide a compressed MP4 and an optional poster image.
2. In `_data/research.yml` update the matching `video.youtube_id` (only the 11-character ID) or set `video.mp4` to the actual local path.
3. The large video panel will automatically replace the placeholder on the corresponding flagship project page.
4. Send the link or video file, and use the actual frames to finalize the labels and thumbnail. The sequence and text above are editable suggestions until the clips have been reviewed.
